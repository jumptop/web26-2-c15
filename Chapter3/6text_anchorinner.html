The morning began in a completely ordinary way, with a quiet room, a half-open window, and several objects sitting on a table without any particular reason for being there. A notebook was placed beside a glass of water, while a small cable stretched across the corner of the desk and disappeared behind a monitor. Nothing important was happening, and there was no particular event waiting to occur. The clock continued moving forward one second at a time, exactly as clocks generally do, while the light outside slowly changed as clouds passed through the sky. Somewhere in another room, a chair remained exactly where someone had left it earlier. A box contained several smaller boxes, each containing objects that were probably useful at some point but were currently doing absolutely nothing. A pen rolled slightly when the table moved, stopped near the edge, and remained there. Someone could have picked it up, but nobody did. The situation required no explanation, because there was nothing especially interesting to explain. As the day continued, various small things happened without forming any meaningful pattern. A door opened and later closed. A computer displayed information, replaced it with different information, and eventually returned to something similar to what it had shown before. Several files existed inside several folders, and some of those folders contained even more folders. Their names suggested that they might have a purpose, although understanding that purpose was unnecessary for the current situation. Eventually, the afternoon arrived simply because enough time had passed since the morning. The objects on the desk were still there, although a few had moved slightly. The glass contained less water than before, the notebook had gained a few lines of writing, and the monitor continued producing light. None of these details led to a conclusion, revealed a hidden message, or represented anything particularly significant. They were simply things that happened while time continued moving forward, which is more or less what time tends to do.

There was once a hallway that connected one side of a building to another side of the same building. People occasionally walked through it because that was generally more convenient than attempting to reach the other side by some unnecessarily complicated route. The walls were painted a color that could reasonably be described as a color, and the floor was made from a material suitable for being walked on. Several lights were attached to the ceiling, providing enough illumination for people to see where they were going. At the end of the hallway stood a table. The table had four legs, a flat surface, and no remarkable characteristics beyond those normally associated with tables. On top of it sat three books, an empty cup, two pieces of paper, and an object whose original purpose was unclear. Nobody seemed particularly concerned about the object. It remained there for several days and possibly longer, contributing very little to the overall operation of the building. Outside, vehicles moved along a road in both directions. Some traveled quickly, others slowly, and several stopped whenever the traffic lights indicated that stopping was appropriate. Trees stood beside the road and moved slightly whenever the wind passed through their branches. Occasionally a bird landed somewhere nearby, looked around for a short period of time, and then departed for reasons known primarily to the bird. Meanwhile, inside the building, computers continued performing calculations that were probably more meaningful than anything described in this paragraph. Data moved from one place to another, buttons were pressed, windows were opened and closed, and people occasionally stared at screens while trying to remember what they had intended to do several minutes earlier. Eventually someone walked through the hallway, noticed the table, ignored the mysterious object, and continued toward another room. Nothing dramatic happened afterward. The hallway remained a hallway, the table continued functioning as a table, and the mysterious object continued to be mysterious mostly because nobody cared enough to investigate it. The day progressed normally until it became evening, at which point the building became quieter. The lights remained on for a while, then some of them were turned off. This marked the end of an entirely unremarkable sequence of events that contained many words but very little useful information.
